# Story and Script Design

Write stories that create playable decisions, not just paragraphs between levels.
Match genre, tone, audience, requested duration and production constraints.

## Start from the Available Material

- **Complete script:** preserve characters, causality, required scenes and endings;
  translate scenes into playable beats. Propose changes explicitly.
- **Partial script or premise:** preserve supplied facts; fill missing motivation,
  transitions and payoff with clearly marked proposals.
- **Requirements only:** propose a logline, protagonist, desire, obstacle, stakes
  and ending, then draft a short beginning-to-end synopsis before chapter detail.
- **Non-narrative brief:** design round/progression flow without forcing dialogue,
  a protagonist or a dramatic arc that the game does not need.

Maintain a small canon record: characters and their goals, relationships, world
rules, established events and unresolved threads. Separate user facts from authored
additions. Ask only about choices that materially block the requested experience.

## Build Cause and Effect

Arrange setup -> complication -> discovery/decision -> consequence -> payoff.
Give each beat a reason to follow the previous one. Turn exposition into an action,
observation, object or concise conversation when possible. Do not add characters,
locations or cinematics without a playable purpose and an asset cost.

For each scene or beat specify:

| Element | Question |
|---|---|
| Time and place | When and where does it occur? How does the player arrive? |
| Cast and intent | Who is present, what do they know, and what do they want? |
| Player agency | What can the player do or choose, rather than only watch? |
| Change | What new knowledge, item, relationship or world state results? |
| Presentation | Gameplay, dialogue, environmental storytelling or short cinematic? |
| Transition | What makes the next beat available? What happens on failure? |

Write actual sample dialogue for key conversations: short lines, distinct voices,
choice text and consequences. Do not substitute 'a conversation happens' for a
script. Make branches change a stated outcome; mark cosmetic choices as cosmetic.

## Place Beats on a Timeline

Use estimated elapsed play minutes separately from fictional dates or times.
Split chapters at meaningful changes in goal, location, knowledge, challenge or
emotional tone, not at arbitrary equal-length intervals. Alternate action,
exploration and reflection when the genre supports it.

Give every chapter a hook, playable goal, meaningful change and exit. Specify
branch destinations, convergence, checkpoints and retry behavior. Preview later
chapter needs without fully scripting every optional exchange up front.

## Check the Story before Generation

Trace required script beats to chapter IDs. Verify item ownership, location,
character knowledge and world changes across transitions. Ensure no branch needs
an item or fact it could never acquire, and that retry cannot duplicate rewards.
Check that the ending addresses the initial goal and that the asset/page manifest
covers every staged character, environment and interaction.
